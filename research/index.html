Research

I do applied ML research at UFMG's SPEED Lab, advised by Prof. Wagner Meira Jr. and Prof. Gisele Pappa. My work so far covers language models, deep learning for incomplete tabular data and clinical ML. Below, each paper comes with a plain-language summary, why it matters, and what I did.

  1. AAAI ICWSM 2026

    Mapping the Political Discourse in the Brazilian Chamber of Deputies: A Multi-Faceted Computational Approach

    Flávio Soriano, Victoria F. Mello, Pedro B. Rigueira, Gisele L. Pappa, Wagner Meira Jr., Ana Paula Couto da Silva, Jussara M. Almeida

    International Conference on Web and Social Media · presented in Los Angeles

    In short
    We read 453,280 speeches from Brazil's Chamber of Deputies (2003–2025) through three lenses: how deputies speak, what they talk about, and who sounds alike. Speeches got shorter and simpler, the agenda swings with national crises, and regional and gender identity often bring deputies together as much as party does.
    Why it matters
    A scalable way to study legislatures beyond votes, and a pipeline that works for any large body of text that changes over time.
    My role
    Stylometric and topic analyses, writing, diagrams and final analysis; I presented the paper.

  2. KDMiLe 2026First author

    The Role of Retained-Layer Representations in Data Selection for Compressed Transformer Fine-Tuning

    Victoria F. Mello, Flávio Soriano, Pedro B. Rigueira, Gisele L. Pappa, Wagner Meira Jr., Washington Cunha, Marcos André Gonçalves

    Symposium on Knowledge Discovery, Mining and Learning · Brazilian Computer Society · 25% acceptance · in Portuguese

    In short
    When you remove layers from BERT to make fine-tuning cheaper, which examples should you train it on? Choosing them from the point of view of the layers you keep gave the best accuracy among compressed variants, with about 69% less compute and 13–19% less GPU memory than the full model.
    Why it matters
    Model size and data size are usually tuned separately; this shows they interact, which matters whenever adapting models has a real cost.
    My role
    First author: method, implementation, experiments and writing.

  1. BRACIS 2025

    TRIDENT: Tabular Representation Inference with Dedicated Embeddings for Null Tokens

    Pedro B. Rigueira, Victoria F. Mello, Guilherme H. G. Evangelista, Caio S. Grossi, Giovana A. M. Machado, Pedro Dutenhefner, Wagner Meira Jr., Gisele L. Pappa

    Brazilian Conference on Intelligent Systems, Brazil's main AI conference · Springer LNAI

    In short
    A Transformer for tables that reads missing values instead of filling them in, using learned "missing" tokens and self-supervised pre-training. From 40% missing data onward it beats six strong baselines, by up to 3.4 points of macro-F1.
    Why it matters
    In health, credit and sensor data, gaps are the norm; a model that degrades gracefully needs fewer fragile imputation pipelines.
    My role
    Architecture, implementation, the Optuna hyperparameter-optimization framework and the baseline comparison.

  2. CinC 2025

    From Signals to Graphs: A Novel Approach for Intelligent ECG Audit

    Guilherme H. G. Evangelista, Pedro B. Rigueira, Victoria F. Mello, et al.

    Computing in Cardiology

    In short
    We turn ECGs into graphs and combine graph and time-series features to find the exams most worth a specialist’s review, making audits more than four times as efficient as random sampling.
    Why it matters
    Expert review time is scarce; spending it where the signal is most ambiguous gets more out of every audit.
    My role
    Ideation, implementation, experiments and writing.

  3. ENIAC 2025

    Optimizing Reduced-Lead ECG Diagnosis: An Interpretable Pipeline for Lead Selection and Model Adaptation

    Luisa G. Porfírio*, Guilherme H. G. Evangelista*, Pedro B. Rigueira*, Caio S. Grossi*, Artur Xavier*, Victoria A. F. de Mello*, Raquel Teodoro*, et al.

    National Meeting on Artificial and Computational Intelligence · Brazilian Computer Society · *equal contribution

    In short
    We rank ECG leads with several interpretability methods and adapt a CNN + Transformer to use only the best ones. With just two leads it matches the full 12-lead model (macro-F1 0.885).
    Why it matters
    Wearables record only a few leads; this is a step toward clinical-grade heart diagnosis at home and in low-resource settings.
    My role
    Equal-contribution co-author.

  1. ENIAC 2024

    Identification of Sepsis Subphenotypes via ICU Data Clustering

    Giovana Assis, Victoria F. Mello, Haniel B. Ribeiro, Alexandre G. Barros, Gisele L. Pappa, Wagner Meira Jr.

    Brazilian Computer Society · in Portuguese

    In short
    Clustering ICU records of sepsis patients from MIMIC-IV revealed five clinically distinct subgroups, a finer picture than the standard severity score.
    Why it matters
    Sepsis is many diseases in one; subphenotypes are a step toward more personalized treatment.
